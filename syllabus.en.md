Course Description:
This course provides a foundational and interdisciplinary understanding of leadership. Current issues
in leadership and case studies are also discussed. Students will also develop their own philosophy of
leadership to help them become problem-solvers for application to enhancing the effectiveness of
SDGs.

Learning Objectives:
The purpose of this course is to provide an overview of leadership theory and its application that
crosses various fields to help solve problems for the SDGs initiative. Since the SDGs are
fundamentally about creating a more just and equitable world, where all people have the opportunity
to thrive, the purpose of this course studying in English particularly is to understand the SDGs
knowledge and problem-solving skills in English and develop a sense of global citizenship for
fostering an appreciation for diverse cultures, perspectives, and experiences.

Thus, through practice of English-speaking work environment and cross-cultural interactions in class,
the purpose of this course for graduate students to also learn to reflect and cope with some of the
communication difficulties involved in conducting business with partners from different cultural
backgrounds.

Learner-Centered Outcomes:
Upon completion of this course, the successful student should be able to:

1.   Recognize key concepts and major theories of leadership.
2.   Examine different leadership styles and approaches, comparing their effectiveness in various
     contexts.
3.   Formulate a leadership vision that reflects personal goals and values within the context of the SDGs.
4.   Suggest leadership solutions for global issues in their own field of expertise, incorporating
     diversity and ethical perspectives.

                 Essay Assignment Instruction: Personal Leadership Philosophy

This essay should follow a clear five-paragraph structure, focusing on how your leadership
philosophy has evolved through personal experience.

♦    Introduction
    • Introduce your current idea of what leadership means to you.
    • Briefly mention key experiences that shaped this idea.
    • End with a thesis statement explaining how your leadership philosophy has developed over
         time.

♦   Body Paragraph 1: A Turning Point Before Entering University
   • Describe a specific event or moment before university that first made you think about
       leadership.
   • Where and when did it happen? Who was involved?
   • What did you feel and learn? How did it shape your early values (e.g., responsibility,
       empathy, fairness)?
Example: An experience in middle or high school when you took responsibility in a team project or
supported a friend in need.

♦   Body Paragraph 2: A Leadership Experience During University Life
   • Choose a specific situation in university where you had to show leadership (formal or
      informal).
   • Describe the context, your actions, the outcome, and how your feelings changed through the
      experience.
   • What did you learn about yourself and others through this experience?
Example: Organizing an event, managing a group project, or supporting a struggling teammate.

♦    Body Paragraph 3: Influential Relationships That Shaped Your Values this Semester
    • Write about a leadership theory/concept you learned this semester and a person who practiced
       it in a way that significantly influenced your leadership philosophy (e.g., a family member,
       teacher, senior, supervisor, or peer).
    • What did this person do or say that influenced you?
    • Looking back, which leadership theory or concept best explains why their influence was
       meaningful to you?
